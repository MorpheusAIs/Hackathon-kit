# GTM wedge

Order matters: ICP and wedge first, funnel last.

1. **ICP:** one sentence naming the exact first buyer (role, business type, size, region, trigger moment). "Owner of a 3–10 truck plumbing company in Texas who loses work to missed calls" is the bar. "SMBs" fails.
2. **Wedge:** the single narrow job you do better than anything they use today, and why AI makes it possible now.
3. **Alternatives:** what they do today (including "nothing" or "a spreadsheet") and what it costs them.
4. **Cost of inaction:** what the buyer loses each month if nothing changes (money, time, risk, or a non-economic harm; say which). This anchors the price.
5. **Price:** a number per month or per use, justified by the cost of the alternative and the cost of inaction. Price to willingness to pay; do not price to cost.
6. **Viability:** who pays, the rough unit cost on Morpheus per use versus the price, and whether the build can be sustained after the event.
7. **Network angle:** does each user bring the next one (referrals, two-sided, shared data)? If yes, say how. If no, say so.
8. **First 10 users:** where exactly they are (subreddit, Facebook group, trade association, a named local meetup) and the message you send them. Draft that message.
9. **Evidence test before judging:** the site's honest "notify me" signup, the target number, and how you'll count it. Plus at most one disclosed community question, posted by the approver. Signups are interest, not proof of payment.
10. **Theme line:** one sentence linking this to the event's theme, from CLAUDE.md.

## Output: `research/gtm.md`
At most one page, using these 10 headings. Each claim cites `research/` or a URL.
```
# GTM: <product, one line>
## ICP
## Wedge
## Alternatives
## Cost of inaction
## Price
## Viability
## Network angle
## First 10 users
## Evidence test
## Theme line
```

## Done when
- All 10 headings are present, in this order.
- Every claim cites `research/` or a URL.
- The file is one page; the ICP is one named buyer and the price is justified by the alternative's cost and the cost of inaction.
